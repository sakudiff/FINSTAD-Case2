\section{Portfolio Optimization}

Mean-variance optimization establishes the mathematical foundation for modern asset allocation by identifying the locus of portfolios that maximize expected return for a defined level of variance, or equivalently, minimize variance for a target expected return. This section formulates the optimization framework, derives the analytical and numerical solutions for the Global Minimum Variance (GMV) and Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) portfolios, and provides an extensive econometric interpretation of the optimal asset weightings and the Efficient Frontier.

\subsection{Mathematical Framework and Convex Optimization Formulation}

Let the asset universe consist of $n = 6$ constituents with annualized expected return vector $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and annualized symmetric positive semi-definite covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. Portfolio allocations are represented by the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = [w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n]^T \in \mathbb{R}^n$. All allocations are restricted to the unit simplex $\Delta^{n-1} = \{ \mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n : \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \; w_i \ge 0 \; \forall i \}$, enforcing full investment and strictly prohibiting short sales.

\subsubsection{Global Minimum Variance Portfolio Formulation}

The Global Minimum Variance portfolio represents the leftmost boundary point of the Markowitz bullet, minimizing total portfolio variance without regard to expected return. Formally, the primal quadratic program is defined as:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \quad w_i \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, n\}
\end{equation}

In the unconstrained benchmark case that permits short sales, the Lagrangian function is given by $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \lambda) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} - \lambda (\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} - 1)$. Setting first-order stationarity conditions $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} - \lambda \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$ yields the classic analytical solution:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}_{\text{GMV, unconstrained}}^* = \frac{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \mathbf{1}}, \quad \sigma_{\text{GMV}}^2 = \frac{1}{\mathbf{1}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \mathbf{1}}
\end{equation}

Under the long-only constraint ($w_i \ge 0$), the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker first-order optimality conditions require:

\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}^* - \lambda \mathbf{1} - \boldsymbol{\nu}^* = \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{w}^* \ge \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{w}^* = 1, \quad \boldsymbol{\nu}^* \ge \mathbf{0}, \quad \nu_i^* w_i^* = 0 \quad \forall i
\end{equation}

where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the budget constraint, and $\boldsymbol{\nu}^* \in \mathbb{R}_+^n$ is the vector of dual Lagrange multipliers enforcing the non-negativity bounds. When an asset allocation is constrained to zero ($w_i^* = 0$), its corresponding dual multiplier $\nu_i^* > 0$ represents the shadow cost, measuring the marginal variance penalty that would be incurred if a non-zero allocation were forced upon the portfolio.

\subsubsection{Maximum Sharpe Ratio Portfolio Formulation}

The Tangency portfolio identifies the unique coordinate along the Efficient Frontier that maximizes excess return per unit of total risk relative to the Philippine 91-Day Treasury Bill baseline yield ($R_f = 5.25\%$ per annum):

\begin{equation}
  \max_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f}{\sqrt{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}}} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \quad w_i \ge 0 \quad \forall i
\end{equation}

Because the Sharpe ratio objective is non-linear and fractional, it can be mapped into an equivalent strictly convex quadratic program \parencite{sharpe1964}. Applying the variable transformation $\mathbf{y} = \kappa \mathbf{w}$ with scaling factor $\kappa = (\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f)^{-1} > 0$, the optimization maps to:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{y}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{y} \quad \text{subject to} \quad (\boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f \mathbf{1})^T \mathbf{y} = 1, \quad \mathbf{y} \ge \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}

Upon solving for the optimal vector $\mathbf{y}^*$ via quadratic programming, the normalized portfolio weights are recovered through the relation $\mathbf{w}_{\text{Tangency}}^* = \mathbf{y}^* / (\mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{y}^*)$.

Table~\ref{tab:optimal_weights} details the numerical weights, annualized returns, annualized standard deviations, and Sharpe ratios for the Global Minimum Variance and Maximum Sharpe Ratio portfolios solved via the Sequential Least Squares Programming and Goldfarb-Idnani dual quadratic programming algorithms.

The empirical weights in Table~\ref{tab:optimal_weights} demonstrate how mathematical optimization exploits cross-asset covariance structures to satisfy divergent investment objectives.

The Global Minimum Variance portfolio allocates 79.09 percent of its capital to domestic Philippine equities, allocating 31.01 percent to PLDT Inc. (\texttt{TEL}), 31.23 percent to Manila Electric Company (\texttt{MER}), and 16.85 percent to Aboitiz Equity Ventures (\texttt{AEV}). This concentration stems directly from the low standalone volatilities of Philippine utility and telecom cash flows, where annualized standard deviations remain bounded at 31.13 percent for \texttt{TEL}, 31.38 percent for \texttt{MER}, and 38.91 percent for \texttt{AEV}, measured as the square root of the annualized covariance-matrix diagonal used in the optimization (simple daily returns annualized on a 252-day convention). Under the Euler risk decomposition theorem, the marginal contribution to risk must be equalized across all active positions in the minimum-variance state. Consequently, the optimizer pairs these domestic assets to minimize diagonal variance terms while exploiting low inter-asset correlations ($r_{\text{TEL, MER}} = 0.1187$, $r_{\text{TEL, AEV}} = 0.0884$, $r_{\text{MER, AEV}} = 0.2959$).

A notable empirical finding is the 3.15 percent allocation assigned to NVIDIA Corporation (\texttt{NVDA}) within the GMV portfolio. Despite NVIDIA exhibiting a high standalone volatility of 53.18 percent, its pairwise correlation with domestic Philippine equities is exceptionally low, registering at 0.1894 with \texttt{TEL} and 0.0121 with \texttt{MER}. In matrix terms, the marginal risk derivative $\partial \sigma_p / \partial w_i = (\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w})_i / \sigma_p$ demonstrates that introducing an uncorrelated foreign asset dampens aggregate portfolio variance because the off-diagonal covariance savings outweigh the small diagonal variance contribution. The same mechanism admits \texttt{META} (12.86 percent) and \texttt{BTC} (4.90 percent) at modest weights: their correlations with the domestic block are near zero (MER--META 0.0172, MER--BTC 0.0286), offsetting their higher diagonal variances. The resulting GMV portfolio delivers an annualized expected return of 16.03 percent with an annualized volatility of 20.22 percent, achieving a Sharpe ratio of 0.5332.

In contrast, the Maximum Sharpe Ratio portfolio shifts capital aggressively into high-momentum growth engines. The tangency vector allocates 55.44 percent to NVIDIA Corporation (\texttt{NVDA}), 26.14 percent to Bitcoin (\texttt{BTC}), and 18.42 percent to Manila Electric Company (\texttt{MER}). The heavy concentration in \texttt{NVDA} and \texttt{BTC} reflects the compounding capital appreciation experienced by US large-capitalization technology and cryptocurrency assets during the sample period, which expands the expected excess return numerator $(\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f)$ at a rate that far outpaces the expansion of portfolio variance in the denominator.

Manila Electric Company (\texttt{MER}) serves as the sole domestic anchor within the Maximum Sharpe portfolio, capturing nearly a fifth of total allocation. \texttt{MER} combines a strong historical return trajectory with utility dividend stability and near-zero correlation with global technology equities ($r_{\text{MER, NVDA}} = 0.0121$, $r_{\text{MER, BTC}} = 0.0286$). By incorporating \texttt{MER}, the optimizer mitigates currency and equity volatility shocks without diluting overall return generation. Telecommunications (\texttt{TEL}) and industrial conglomerates (\texttt{AEV}) are excluded from the tangency portfolio because their lower expected returns (12.96 percent and -5.26 percent annualized) dilute the Sharpe numerator, and Meta Platforms (\texttt{META}) receives zero weight despite its 28.99 percent expected return: at 43.82 percent volatility and a 0.5333 correlation with \texttt{NVDA}, its risk-adjusted contribution lies below the tangency line defined by \texttt{NVDA}, \texttt{BTC}, and \texttt{MER}. The resulting Maximum Sharpe portfolio achieves an annualized expected return of 58.10 percent and an annualized volatility of 38.25 percent, achieving the highest Sharpe ratio among the optimized portfolios at 1.3815.

The geometric properties displayed in Figure~\ref{fig:efficient_frontier} illustrate the core principles of portfolio efficiency. The efficient frontier hyperbola originates at the GMV coordinate $(\sigma = 20.22\%, \mu = 16.03\%)$ and curves upward with diminishing marginal returns to incremental volatility, terminating at the maximum-return boundary. The Maximum Sharpe portfolio $(\sigma = 38.25\%, \mu = 58.10\%)$ marks the tangency point of the risk-free rate line with the risky asset envelope: under the separation theorem, an investor can move along the line connecting the 5.25 percent risk-free rate to this portfolio through risk-free lending or borrowing, and the slope of that line equals the maximum Sharpe ratio (1.3815). The figure plots only the frontier and the two optimal portfolios; individual assets and the Capital Allocation Line are not shown.
