\section{Exploratory Financial Analysis}

Exploratory financial analysis establishes the empirical foundation required to construct robust portfolio optimization algorithms and risk management frameworks. This section evaluates central tendency, volatility dynamics, distributional shape, tail risk diagnostics, and pairwise asset return co-movements across the 1,508 daily log return rows of the master panel spanning January 2, 2020 through January 2, 2026. Assets with a complete price record (TEL, NVDA, META, BTC, SPY) contribute 1,508 observed daily returns, while the PSE-sourced instruments contribute 1,421 (MER) and 1,420 (AEV) observed returns, the shortfall reflecting days without captured PSE prices: Philippine holidays falling on US trading days, genuine market closures (the two-day suspension of March 17--18, 2020, calamity- and election-driven closures, and a systems-glitch cancellation on January 4, 2022), a single per-stock no-trade day for AEV on October 25, 2022, and one source-data gap on October 24, 2022, when the PSE was open but no PSE EDGE records were captured for either domestic name. All statistics below are computed on observed returns only; no-trade days are never assigned a zero return, and returns that span a no-trade gap are measured against the last traded close so the full move appears in the sample.

\subsection{Empirical Descriptive Statistics and Risk Return Profiles}

Table~\ref{tab:desc_stats} summarizes the descriptive statistics computed from the daily log return series across all seven assets.

\input{tables/tbl_desc_stats.tex}

The results reveal pronounced divergence in expected return and risk parameters across asset classes. NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) generated the highest average daily log return at 0.0023, an annualized mean of roughly 57.7 percent. Bitcoin (BTC) followed at 0.0017 per day (42.7 percent annualized), with Meta Platforms (META) and the SPDR S\&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY) recording moderate means of 0.0008 and 0.0006. The Philippine universe displayed subdued central tendencies: Manila Electric Company (MER) averaged 0.0003 per day (8.0 percent annualized) and PLDT Inc. (TEL) 0.0003 (8.1 percent). Aboitiz Equity Ventures (AEV) was the only asset with a negative mean return, at -0.0005 per day (-13.6 percent annualized).

Daily volatility metrics confirm the expected risk hierarchy. Bitcoin exhibited the highest daily standard deviation of 0.0393 (62.3 percent annualized), followed by NVIDIA at 0.0333 (52.9 percent) and Meta at 0.0279 (44.2 percent). Aboitiz Equity Ventures registered the most volatile Philippine series at 0.0253 (40.2 percent annualized), while the lower-volatility domestic names TEL (PSE Services) and MER (PSE Industrial) recorded 0.0199 (31.5 percent annualized) and 0.0204 (32.3 percent annualized) respectively. The benchmark SPY displayed the lowest daily volatility at 0.0131 (20.8 percent annualized), reflecting the risk reduction achieved through broad market diversification.\footnote{PSE EDGE closing prices are unadjusted for corporate actions. Ex-dividend days therefore appear as small single-day price dips, on the order of 0.5 percent per quarterly dividend for MER, which marginally inflates measured daily volatility and kurtosis for the domestic names.}

Third and fourth statistical moments document departure from Gaussian normality, with severity that is heterogeneous across the universe. Skewness is negative for TEL (-1.7687), BTC (-1.2226), META (-1.0731), SPY (-0.5588), and mildly negative for the PSE names once gap-crossing moves are included, MER (-0.2346) and AEV (-0.2215); NVDA is approximately symmetric (0.0434). Negative skew indicates that extreme negative drawdowns occur with greater magnitude than positive surges for those assets.

Kurtosis is reported throughout in its excess form, with a Gaussian baseline of zero. All seven assets are leptokurtic, but the domestic instruments are far closer to the Gaussian benchmark than the global block. TEL is the most extreme at 31.4844, driven largely by idiosyncratic single-day events, notably the -27.0 percent daily return of December 19, 2022. META (22.0943), BTC (16.2966), and SPY (13.3999) follow, while MER (5.7962), NVDA (4.0565), and AEV (3.7706) exhibit moderate excess kurtosis. The heavy tails confirm that standard mean-variance assumptions understate tail risk, but the corrected PSE-based series show that the domestic names are materially better behaved than the earlier vendor-sourced data suggested.

The trajectories exhibit pronounced volatility clustering: large return shocks of either sign are followed by large shocks, while tranquil periods persist in distinct bands. The March 2020 systemic liquidity shock was the defining event of the sample. Measured close-to-close from February 19 to March 23, 2020, the crisis losses were severe and uneven: TEL -9.3 percent, MER -22.1 percent, AEV -41.4 percent, NVDA -32.4 percent, META -31.9 percent, BTC -33.4 percent, and SPY -33.7 percent. The PSE-sourced series show honest gaps rather than fabricated flat days on dates without captured prices: Philippine holidays falling on US trading days, the two-day PSE market suspension of March 17--18, 2020, AEV's sole per-stock no-trade day on October 25, 2022, and a source-data gap on October 24, 2022. The exchange reopened on March 19 with shortened trading hours, and both MER and AEV printed prices that day; their reopening returns are measured against the last pre-suspension close, so the full gap moves appear in the series (MER -16.7 percent, AEV -11.8 percent). The largest domestic single-day extremes of the sample were AEV's -16.8 percent trough on April 16, 2020, MER's -16.7 percent re-open on March 19, 2020, and TEL's -27.0 percent on December 19, 2022.

Sector-specific regimes followed in subsequent years. The 2022 monetary tightening cycle produced sustained dispersion in technology and crypto assets, with Meta posting a -30.6 percent single-day return on February 3, 2022 after its earnings release and calendar-year losses of -64.2 percent for both META and BTC and -50.3 percent for NVDA. The domestic names absorbed the same cycle far more gently, with calendar-year 2022 returns of -31.7 percent for TEL, -7.6 percent for MER, and -3.3 percent for AEV. TEL's idiosyncratic -27.0 percent shock of December 19, 2022 stands out as the largest domestic move of the sample and is visible in the lower tail of its return distribution.

\subsection{Distributional Shape and Non-Normality Diagnostics}

Figure~\ref{fig:density_hist} compares the empirical kernel density distributions of daily log returns against fitted Gaussian reference curves.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \safeincludegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig-density-hist-1.pdf}

  \label{fig:density_hist}

The empirical density overlays show the classic departure pattern: central peaks that rise above the theoretical normal, shoulders that collapse faster than the Gaussian, and tails that extend much farther outward. The effect is strongest for TEL, BTC, META, and SPY, consistent with their excess kurtosis values, and mildest for NVDA, AEV, and MER, whose empirical densities track the Gaussian far more closely while still exhibiting wider tails than the reference curve.

Figure~\ref{fig:qq_plots} provides normal Quantile-Quantile (Q-Q) diagnostics for tail behavior.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \safeincludegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig-qq-plots-1.pdf}
  \caption{Normal Q-Q Diagnostics Demonstrating Heavy Tail Leptokurtosis}
  \label{fig:qq_plots}
\end{figure}

The Q-Q plots display S-shaped departures from the theoretical reference line across the universe. Sample quantiles bend sharply below the line in the lower left quadrant and above it in the upper right, confirming that realized tails are fatter than normality implies. The curvature is most pronounced for TEL and the global block, while NVDA, AEV, and MER show milder, though still visible, tail divergence. Risk models that assume Gaussian returns will underestimate tail risk across the portfolio, and the effect is largest in the technology and cryptocurrency positions.

The logarithmic plot reveals massive long-run divergence across the universe. NVIDIA compounded USD 1.00 into roughly USD 31.63 by the end of the sample, a total return of 3,062.8 percent, driven by the global expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure demand. Bitcoin reached approximately USD 12.88 (1,187.6 percent) despite a drawdown of more than 60 percent during the 2022 crypto winter, while META reached USD 3.12 (212.2 percent) and SPY USD 2.29 (129.5 percent). The Philippine block delivered modest positive outcomes for TEL and MER, ending at USD 1.63 (62.6 percent) and USD 1.57 (57.2 percent) respectively, the latter recovering from three down years in 2020--2022 to compound strongly through 2023--2025. The divergence demonstrates that high-volatility global assets generated substantial long-run wealth, while the domestic names acted as stability anchors rather than growth engines.

The correlation structure is now estimated on genuinely traded PSE returns, and it paints a materially different picture from the vendor-sourced data. Within the global block, co-movement is strong: NVDA and META correlate at +0.5331, NVDA with SPY at +0.7052, META with SPY at +0.6284, and BTC with SPY at +0.3880. These linkages reflect common exposure to global liquidity and risk sentiment.

The Philippine block displays moderate positive internal co-movement: MER-TEL at +0.1187, AEV-TEL at +0.0884, and MER-AEV at +0.2959. The earlier claim of negative domestic pairwise correlations was an artifact of stale zero-volume OTC quotes and is not supported by the PSE data. What the corrected data do support is a genuine cross-market diversification story: the domestic names are nearly orthogonal to global risk assets. MER correlates +0.0121 with NVDA, +0.0172 with META, +0.0286 with BTC, and +0.0518 with SPY; AEV records +0.0155, +0.0356, +0.0155, and +0.0904 against the same four series. TEL, whose NYSE-listed ADR (PHI) trades in US hours, shows the strongest international linkage of the domestic set, at +0.3010 with SPY and +0.1895 with NVDA.

These near-zero cross-market coefficients provide the quantitative justification for combining high-return international technology and crypto assets with Philippine equities. The diversification benefit is real and now rests on clean data: domestic positions add variance-reduction potential with essentially no correlation drag from global tech and crypto shocks, while the moderate internal correlations among TEL, MER, and AEV preserve a degree of local systematic risk that sectoral selection can further diversify.
