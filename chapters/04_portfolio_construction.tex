\section{Portfolio Construction and Strategy Evaluation}

Portfolio construction transforms individual asset return dynamics into integrated allocation strategies. This section evaluates the performance of equal-weighted multi-asset portfolios under two execution regimes: an un-rebalanced Buy-and-Hold strategy and a Monthly Rebalanced strategy. Both start from an equal allocation vector ($w_i = 1/6 \approx 16.67$ percent) across the six-asset universe (TEL, MER, AEV, NVDA, META, BTC) on January 2, 2020 and are benchmarked against the SPDR S\&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY). Portfolio arithmetic uses a carried price series at the aggregation layer: days when the PSE is closed contribute no return, and resumption days capture the full move across the gap. The stored master dataset itself contains no imputed rows, as documented in the data acquisition chapter. Portfolio returns are computed on simple (arithmetic) daily returns and compounded directly, so terminal wealth equals the value of the actual share-weighted holdings.

Both portfolio configurations generated substantial alpha relative to the SPY benchmark. The Buy-and-Hold strategy delivered a cumulative return of 754.79 percent and a 43.13 percent CAGR, nearly six times the benchmark's cumulative return. This outperformance was driven by unconstrained weight compounding in NVIDIA and Bitcoin, which together absorbed roughly 87 percent of the portfolio's capital by the end of the sample. The cost of that concentration is visible in the risk metrics: annualized volatility of 35.11 percent and a maximum drawdown of -58.21 percent, the deepest in the table.

Monthly rebalancing re-engineered the risk-return trade-off. The disciplined portfolio returned 387.38 percent cumulatively (30.30 percent CAGR), surrendering 367.4 percentage points of cumulative return relative to Buy-and-Hold but cutting annualized volatility by 10.94 percentage points to 24.17 percent and trimming the maximum drawdown by 15.01 percentage points to -43.20 percent. On a risk-adjusted basis the two strategies are close, with the Buy-and-Hold Sharpe ratio of 1.0787 edging the rebalanced 1.0365; over this particular sample the foregone compounding outweighed the risk saved. Both Sharpe ratios, and both portfolios' absolute returns, remain far ahead of the benchmark's 0.4648 and 129.48 percent.

The trajectories terminate at USD 8.55 (Buy-and-Hold), USD 4.87 (Monthly Rebalanced), and USD 2.29 (SPY). The divergence emerged early: calendar-year 2020 returns were 84.72 percent for Buy-and-Hold, 70.37 percent for the rebalanced portfolio, and 17.24 percent for SPY, followed by 2021 gains of 54.93 percent, 46.80 percent, and 28.73 percent respectively. The rebalanced portfolio systematically realized profits from the surging technology and crypto positions at each monthly reset and redeployed the proceeds into the lagging domestic names, which kept its wealth path smoother but also capped its participation in the 2020--2021 bull market.

The 2022 tightening cycle was the stress test that separated the regimes. Buy-and-Hold lost 49.57 percent in calendar 2022 and carved out its maximum drawdown of -58.21 percent from its November 8, 2021 peak to a trough on October 14, 2022, as NVIDIA (-50.3 percent), Meta (-64.2 percent), and Bitcoin (-64.2 percent) collapsed simultaneously while even TEL fell 31.7 percent. The rebalanced portfolio absorbed the same shock with a 36.77 percent calendar-year loss and a -43.20 percent drawdown that bottomed only two trading days earlier, on October 12, 2022, from a peak on December 27, 2021. Monthly profit-taking and redeployment into the comparatively resilient Philippine names (MER -7.6 percent, AEV -3.3 percent in 2022) therefore did not shorten the drawdown, which the 2023 recovery resolved for both strategies at roughly the same time; what rebalancing bought was depth, shaving 15.01 percentage points off the trough. SPY, by contrast, lost 18.18 percent in 2022 with a peak-to-trough drawdown of -33.72 percent confined to the March 2020 COVID crash.

The recovery phase reinforced the same pattern. In 2023 the Buy-and-Hold portfolio returned 125.47 percent and in 2024 a further 116.05 percent, powered by the AI-driven re-rating of NVIDIA and the crypto recovery; the rebalanced portfolio returned 85.09 percent and 50.88 percent over the same two years, and SPY 26.18 percent and 24.89 percent. In 2025 the rebalanced portfolio's diversified exposure lagged, returning 9.78 percent against 19.92 percent for Buy-and-Hold and 17.72 percent for SPY, as the domestic block and Bitcoin detracted. Over the full window, disciplined rebalancing traded roughly 1.76x of terminal wealth for a materially calmer path.

The drift visualizer exposes the core vulnerability of passive execution. Each asset began at a 16.67 percent weight on January 2, 2020. Differential performance then restructured the allocation completely: by the end of the sample, NVIDIA had grown to 61.7 percent and Bitcoin to 25.1 percent, with META at 6.1 percent, while the three Philippine equities were diluted to 3.2 percent (TEL), 3.1 percent (MER), and 0.9 percent (AEV). The technology-cryptocurrency pair finished at 86.8 percent of the portfolio, and NVIDIA's weight peaked at 62.3 percent in late December 2025. The Buy-and-Hold portfolio thus ended the period as a concentrated two-asset bet with a small domestic hedge, a transformation that explains both its extraordinary cumulative return and its deep drawdown.

Monthly rebalancing prevents this drift by construction: on the first trading day of each month the weights are restored to 16.67 percent, selling the appreciated technology and crypto positions and buying the underweight domestic and lagging assets. This mechanical discipline enforces a systematic contrarian stance that caps concentration risk and keeps the portfolio aligned with its original diversification intent, at the measured cost of lower realized cumulative return over a sample dominated by two assets' exceptional appreciation.
