\section{Introduction}

The multi-asset investment landscape during the sample period from January 2, 2020 through December 31, 2025 presented extraordinary structural regime changes. Global investors weathered the initial black swan liquidity shock of the COVID-19 pandemic, unprecedented fiscal and monetary expansion, subsequent four-decade inflation spikes, aggressive policy rate hikes by the Federal Reserve and Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), an artificial intelligence equity surge, and the institutionalization of digital assets.

Navigating such volatility requires disciplined quantitative portfolio management. Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT), established by Markowitz (1952) and expanded by Sharpe (1964), provides the mathematical framework to optimize return relative to portfolio variance. However, real-world asset returns exhibit heavy tails (leptokurtosis) and volatility clustering, demanding robust downside risk analytics such as Value at Risk (VaR), Expected Shortfall (CVaR), Monte Carlo simulations, and crisis stress testing (Jorion, 2007; Artzner et al., 1999).

\subsection{Risk-Free Rate Benchmark Specification}

The hurdle rate for all excess return, Sharpe ratio, and tangency portfolio calculations is anchored to the Philippine 91-Day Treasury Bill (3-Month T-Bill) secondary market yield. Quoted at an annualized benchmark rate of $R_f = 5.25\%$, it is converted into a daily log-equivalent rate assuming a 252-trading-day convention:

\begin{equation}
  r_{f,\text{daily}} = \frac{\ln(1 + R_f)}{252} = \frac{\ln(1 + 0.0525)}{252} \approx 0.00020307 \quad (0.0203\% \text{ per day})
\end{equation}

This hurdle rate represents the riskless return baseline earned by domestic institutional capital.
