\section{Portfolio Risk Assessment}

Standard deviation measures symmetric return dispersion around the mean, but financial return series frequently exhibit pronounced negative skewness and heavy tails (leptokurtosis). To evaluate downside risk and tail vulnerability beyond second-moment volatility, this section conducts downside risk analytics using Value at Risk (VaR), Expected Shortfall (CVaR), multivariate normal Monte Carlo simulations, and historical crisis stress testing across the Global Minimum Variance (GMV) and Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) portfolios.

\subsection{Mathematical Framework for Tail Risk Measures}

\subsubsection{Value at Risk (VaR)}

Let $L = -\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{r}$ denote the loss variable of a portfolio with allocation vector $\mathbf{w}$ and asset return vector $\mathbf{r}$. The Value at Risk at confidence level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ over a daily horizon is defined as the $\alpha$-quantile of the loss distribution:

\begin{equation}
  \text{VaR}_\alpha(\mathbf{w}) = \inf \left\{ l \in \mathbb{R} : P(L > l) \le 1 - \alpha \right\} = -F_R^{-1}(1 - \alpha)
\end{equation}

where $F_R(r)$ is the cumulative distribution function of portfolio returns. Historical VaR calculates the empirical quantile of observed sample returns without distribution assumptions, whereas Parametric Gaussian VaR assumes a normal distribution:

\begin{equation}
  \text{VaR}_\alpha^{\text{Parametric}}(\mathbf{w}) = -\left( \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{daily}} + z_{1-\alpha} \sqrt{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{daily}} \mathbf{w}} \right)
\end{equation}

where $z_{1-\alpha}$ represents the standard normal quantile corresponding to probability $1 - \alpha$.

\subsubsection{Expected Shortfall (CVaR)}

While VaR measures the boundary threshold of loss, it fails to quantify the magnitude of losses beyond that boundary and lacks mathematical subadditivity under non-normal distributions. Expected Shortfall ($\text{ES}_\alpha$ or Conditional VaR) measures the expected loss conditional on the loss exceeding the $\text{VaR}_\alpha$ threshold:

\begin{equation}
  \text{ES}_\alpha(\mathbf{w}) = \mathbb{E} \left[ L \mid L \ge \text{VaR}_\alpha(\mathbf{w}) \right] = \frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \int_{\alpha}^{1} \text{VaR}_u(\mathbf{w}) \, du
\end{equation}

Expected Shortfall satisfies all four axioms of coherent risk measures established by \textcite{artzner1999}, guaranteeing subadditivity $\text{ES}_\alpha(\mathbf{w}_1 + \mathbf{w}_2) \le \text{ES}_\alpha(\mathbf{w}_1) + \text{ES}_\alpha(\mathbf{w}_2)$ and penalizing fat-tailed risk concentrations.

A rigorous comparative analysis of Table~\ref{tab:var_summary} highlights the downside risk divergence between the two strategies:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{GMV Downside Profile}:
    The GMV portfolio limits daily 95 percent Historical VaR to 1.32 percent, with a corresponding 95 percent Expected Shortfall of 2.14 percent. At the 99 percent confidence level, the GMV portfolio experiences a Historical VaR of 2.45 percent and an Expected Shortfall of 3.25 percent per day. Its Parametric Gaussian 95 percent VaR is 1.48 percent and 99 percent VaR is 2.09 percent. This close alignment between historical and parametric estimates confirms that the defensive domestic asset allocation (\texttt{TEL}, \texttt{AEV}, \texttt{MER}) exhibits mild non-Gaussian tail behavior.

  \item \textbf{Maximum Sharpe Downside Profile}:
    The Maximum Sharpe portfolio incurs significantly severe tail risk due to its 80.96 percent aggregate concentration in \texttt{NVDA} (58.36 percent) and \texttt{META} (22.60 percent). Its daily 95 percent Historical VaR reaches 2.61 percent (nearly double the GMV portfolio), with a 95 percent Expected Shortfall of 4.12 percent. Under extreme 99 percent tail events, the Maximum Sharpe portfolio records a Historical VaR of 4.88 percent and an empirical Expected Shortfall of 6.25 percent per day. Notably, the empirical 99 percent Expected Shortfall (6.25 percent) exceeds the Gaussian parametric estimate (5.38 percent) by 87 basis points, confirming heavy-tailed leptokurtosis driven by technology earnings shocks and macro liquidity contractions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multivariate Monte Carlo Simulation and Distributional Diagnostics}

To model potential return trajectories beyond historical sampling, a 10,000-draw Monte Carlo simulation was conducted. Synthetic return vectors $\mathbf{r}_{\text{sim}} \in \mathbb{R}^6$ were sampled from a multivariate normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{daily}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{daily}})$ using Cholesky decomposition of the annualized covariance matrix:

\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{daily}} = \mathbf{L} \mathbf{L}^T, \quad \mathbf{r}_{\text{sim}} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{daily}} + \mathbf{L} \mathbf{z}, \quad \mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_6)
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:mc_summary} reports the simulated 95 percent VaR and Expected Shortfall metrics derived from the 10,000 synthetic draws.

\input{tables/tbl_mc_summary.tex}

The Monte Carlo simulation yields a 95 percent MC VaR of 1.45 percent and a 95 percent MC ES of 1.82 percent for the GMV portfolio. For the Maximum Sharpe portfolio, the 95 percent MC VaR reaches 3.28 percent with a 95 percent MC ES of 4.11 percent.

\subsection{Crisis Stress Testing and Scenario Analysis}

To evaluate structural resilience against systemic market dislocations, portfolio value impacts were simulated across five historical and hypothetical crisis stress scenarios:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{2020 COVID-19 Crash (Feb 19 -- Mar 23, 2020):} Severe global liquidity shock and market contraction.
  \item \textbf{2022 Crypto Winter (May 5 -- May 13, 2022):} Terra/Luna collapse and digital asset drawdown.
  \item \textbf{2022 Fed Rate Hike Shock (Jun 8 -- Jun 16, 2022):} Monetary policy tightening and interest rate repricing.
  \item \textbf{Tech Sector Selloff (Nov 19, 2021 -- Jan 27, 2022):} High-multiple growth equity valuation compression.
  \item \textbf{Hypothetical Black Swan Event:} A 1.5x multiplier applied to the worst observed historic drawdown across all assets.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{2020 COVID-19 Crash}: The GMV portfolio limited drawdown to -13.82 percent, whereas the Maximum Sharpe portfolio suffered a severe -29.84 percent loss (2.16 times deeper drawdown) due to global equity liquidations.
  \item \textbf{2022 Crypto Winter}: The GMV portfolio incurred a negligible -1.45 percent decline due to zero crypto exposure, while the Maximum Sharpe portfolio lost -12.18 percent.
  \item \textbf{2022 Fed Rate Hike Shock}: The GMV portfolio declined by -3.12 percent compared to -14.65 percent for the Maximum Sharpe portfolio, as higher discount rates compressed tech valuations.
  \item \textbf{Tech Sector Selloff}: The GMV portfolio lost -4.85 percent, whereas the Maximum Sharpe portfolio dropped -22.40 percent due to \texttt{NVDA} and \texttt{META} corrections.
  \item \textbf{Hypothetical Black Swan Event}: The GMV portfolio drawdown was contained at -19.45 percent, while the Maximum Sharpe portfolio experienced a catastrophic -44.76 percent capital loss.
\end{itemize}

These findings demonstrate that while the Maximum Sharpe allocation achieves superior wealth accumulation during economic expansions, the GMV allocation provides non-negotiable capital protection during systemic market crashes.
