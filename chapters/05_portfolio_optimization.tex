\section{Portfolio Optimization}

Mean-variance optimization establishes the mathematical foundation for modern asset allocation by identifying the locus of portfolios that maximize expected return for a defined level of variance, or equivalently, minimize variance for a target expected return. This section formulates the optimization framework, derives the analytical and numerical solutions for the Global Minimum Variance (GMV) and Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) portfolios, and provides an extensive economic and econometric interpretation of the optimal asset weightings, the Efficient Frontier, and the Capital Allocation Line.

\subsection{Mathematical Framework and Convex Optimization Formulation}

Let the asset universe consist of $n = 6$ constituents with annualized expected return vector $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and annualized symmetric positive semi-definite covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. Portfolio allocations are represented by the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = [w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n]^T \in \mathbb{R}^n$. All allocations are restricted to the unit simplex $\Delta^{n-1} = \{ \mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n : \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \; w_i \ge 0 \; \forall i \}$, enforcing full investment and strictly prohibiting short sales.

\subsubsection{Global Minimum Variance (GMV) Portfolio Formulation}

The Global Minimum Variance portfolio represents the leftmost boundary point of the Markowitz bullet, minimizing total portfolio variance without regard to expected return. Formally, the primal quadratic program is defined as:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \quad w_i \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, n\}
\end{equation}

In the unconstrained benchmark case (allowing short sales), the Lagrangian is given by $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \lambda) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} - \lambda (\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} - 1)$. Setting first-order conditions $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} - \lambda \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$ yields the classic analytical solution:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}_{\text{GMV, unconstrained}}^* = \frac{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \mathbf{1}}, \quad \sigma_{\text{GMV}}^2 = \frac{1}{\mathbf{1}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \mathbf{1}}
\end{equation}

Under the long-only constraint ($w_i \ge 0$), the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) first-order optimality conditions require:

\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}^* - \lambda \mathbf{1} - \boldsymbol{\nu}^* = \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{w}^* \ge \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{w}^* = 1, \quad \boldsymbol{\nu}^* \ge \mathbf{0}, \quad \nu_i^* w_i^* = 0 \quad \forall i
\end{equation}

where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ is the Lagrange multiplier for the budget constraint, and $\boldsymbol{\nu}^* \in \mathbb{R}_+^n$ is the vector of dual Lagrange multipliers enforcing the non-negativity bounds. If an asset's non-negativity constraint is active ($w_i^* = 0$), its dual multiplier $\nu_i^* > 0$ represents the shadow cost (the marginal variance penalty incurred if the allocation were forced above zero).

\subsubsection{Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) Portfolio Formulation}

The Tangency portfolio identifies the unique point along the Efficient Frontier that maximizes the Sharpe ratio relative to the Philippine 91-Day Treasury Bill risk-free hurdle rate ($R_f = 5.25\%$ per annum):

\begin{equation}
  \max_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f}{\sqrt{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}}} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \quad w_i \ge 0 \quad \forall i
\end{equation}

Because the Sharpe ratio objective is non-linear and fractional, it can be transformed into an equivalent convex quadratic programming problem \parencite{sharpe1964}. Defining the normalized variable transformation $\mathbf{y} = \kappa \mathbf{w}$ where $\kappa = \frac{1}{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f} > 0$, the problem maps to:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{y}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{y} \quad \text{subject to} \quad (\boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f \mathbf{1})^T \mathbf{y} = 1, \quad \mathbf{y} \ge \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}

Once the optimal vector $\mathbf{y}^*$ is obtained via quadratic programming, the true optimal portfolio weights are recovered through normalization $\mathbf{w}_{\text{Tangency}}^* = \frac{\mathbf{y}^*}{\mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{y}^*}$.

An exhaustive econometric analysis of Table~\ref{tab:optimal_weights} reveals key structural insights into multi-asset diversification:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Global Minimum Variance (GMV) Portfolio Mechanics}:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textit{Dominance of Philippine Regulated Equities}: The GMV portfolio allocates an overwhelming 96.48 percent of total capital to domestic Philippine equities: PLDT Inc. (\texttt{TEL}: 39.82 percent), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (\texttt{AEV}: 31.85 percent), and Manila Electric Company (\texttt{MER}: 24.81 percent). This concentration is driven by their low standalone annualized volatilities ($\sigma_{\text{TEL}} = 18.25\%$, $\sigma_{\text{MER}} = 19.80\%$, $\sigma_{\text{AEV}} = 24.15\%$) and moderate mutual correlations ($r_{\text{TEL, MER}} = 0.45$, $r_{\text{TEL, AEV}} = 0.38$).
      \item \textit{The Cross-Border Diversification Anomaly of NVIDIA}: Despite NVIDIA's high individual volatility ($\sigma_{\text{NVDA}} = 51.20\%$), the GMV optimizer assigns a 3.52 percent weight to \texttt{NVDA}. Under matrix calculus, the marginal contribution to risk is given by $\frac{\partial \sigma_p}{\partial w_i} = \frac{(\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w})_i}{\sigma_p}$. Because the pairwise correlation between \texttt{NVDA} and domestic Philippine equities is exceptionally low ($r_{\text{TEL, NVDA}} = 0.12$, $r_{\text{MER, NVDA}} = 0.15$), the off-diagonal covariance reduction outweighs the diagonal variance contribution, lowering aggregate portfolio risk below what a purely domestic basket could achieve.
      \item \textit{Zero Allocation to High-Beta Growth Assets}: Meta Platforms (\texttt{META}: 0.00 percent) and Bitcoin (\texttt{BTC}: 0.00 percent) are entirely excluded. Bitcoin's extreme annualized volatility of 68.45 percent imposes a quadratic variance penalty ($\sigma_{\text{BTC}}^2 = 0.4685$) that cannot be compensated by covariance dampening, forcing its KKT dual multiplier $\nu_{\text{BTC}}^*$ to bind strictly positive.
      \item \textit{Performance Summary}: The GMV portfolio generates an annualized expected return of 9.42 percent with an annualized volatility of 14.28 percent, achieving a Sharpe ratio of 0.2917 relative to the 5.25 percent risk-free benchmark.
    \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) Portfolio Mechanics}:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textit{High-Growth Tech Engine Allocation}: The Tangency portfolio concentrates 80.96 percent of capital into US technology growth leaders: NVIDIA (\texttt{NVDA}: 58.36 percent) and Meta Platforms (\texttt{META}: 22.60 percent). This aggressive allocation reflects NVIDIA's compounding secular growth during the artificial intelligence expansion cycle, generating a historical return profile that dramatically expands the Sharpe ratio numerator $(\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f)$.
      \item \textit{Domestic Ballast Role of Manila Electric Company}: Manila Electric Company (\texttt{MER}) captures the remaining 19.04 percent allocation. \texttt{MER} combines a solid annualized return with utility cash flow stability and low correlation to global tech equities ($r_{\text{MER, NVDA}} = 0.15$, $r_{\text{MER, META}} = 0.18$). By acting as an uncorrelated volatility stabilizer, \texttt{MER} prevents portfolio variance from expanding excessively, maximizing overall risk-adjusted efficiency.
      \item \textit{Exclusion of Telecoms, Industrials, and Cryptocurrency}: \texttt{TEL} (0.00 percent) and \texttt{AEV} (0.00 percent) are excluded because their lower historical returns dilute the Sharpe numerator. Bitcoin (\texttt{BTC}: 0.00 percent) is excluded because its extreme volatility ($\sigma = 68.45\%$) and severe drawdown profile penalize the Sharpe denominator far more than its return contribution adds to the numerator.
      \item \textit{Performance Summary}: The Maximum Sharpe portfolio achieves an annualized expected return of 49.82 percent and an annualized volatility of 32.14 percent, resulting in a Sharpe ratio of 1.3868 (a 375 percent increase in risk-adjusted efficiency over the GMV portfolio).
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

The geometric structure of Figure~\ref{fig:efficient_frontier} illustrates fundamental portfolio theory principles:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Efficient Frontier Hyperbola}: The curve originates at the GMV coordinate $(\sigma = 14.28\%, \mu = 9.42\%)$ and ascends monotonically with diminishing marginal returns to risk. The horizontal distance between standalone asset markers and the frontier curve visually measures the diversification benefit: standalone assets (such as \texttt{BTC} with $\sigma = 68.45\%$ or \texttt{META} with $\sigma = 46.80\%$) lie far to the right of the efficient locus, demonstrating that holding individual assets unhedged is Pareto-inefficient.
  \item \textbf{Capital Allocation Line (CAL) Tangency}: The Capital Allocation Line originates at the vertical axis intercept $(0, R_f = 5.25\%)$ and forms a precise tangent line to the Efficient Frontier at the Tangency Portfolio $(\sigma = 32.14\%, \mu = 49.82\%)$. The slope of the CAL equals the maximum Sharpe ratio ($S_p = 1.3868$). By combining the Tangency portfolio with lending or borrowing at the risk-free rate, an institutional investor can achieve any point along the CAL, dominating all other risky asset combinations.
\end{itemize}
