\section{Portfolio Optimization}

Mean-variance optimization identifies optimal asset allocations along the Markowitz Efficient Frontier by maximizing expected portfolio return for a given level of variance or minimizing variance for a target expected return.

\subsection{Optimization Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathbf{w} = [w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n]^T$ denote the vector of asset weights across the $n=6$ portfolio constituents, $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ the vector of annualized expected arithmetic returns, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ the $6 \times 6$ annualized asset covariance matrix. All portfolios enforce fully-invested ($\sum_{i=1}^n w_i = 1$) and long-only ($w_i \ge 0$) constraints.

\subsubsection{Global Minimum Variance (GMV) Portfolio}

The Global Minimum Variance portfolio minimizes total portfolio risk regardless of expected return:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf{w}} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \quad w_i \ge 0 \;\; \forall i
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) Portfolio}

The Tangency portfolio maximizes the Sharpe ratio relative to the 5.25 percent risk-free rate hurdle:

\begin{equation}
  \max_{\mathbf{w}} \quad \frac{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} - R_f}{\sqrt{\mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}}} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{1} = 1, \quad w_i \ge 0 \;\; \forall i
\end{equation}

A deep examination of Table~\ref{tab:optimal_weights} reveals structural asset allocation shifts between minimum risk and maximum risk-adjusted return objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Global Minimum Variance Allocation Breakdown}:
    The GMV portfolio allocates 96.48 percent of total capital to low-variance domestic equities: PLDT (\texttt{TEL}: 39.82 percent), Aboitiz Equity Ventures (\texttt{AEV}: 31.85 percent), and Manila Electric (\texttt{MER}: 24.81 percent). NVIDIA (\texttt{NVDA}) receives a minor 3.52 percent weight due to its low pairwise correlation with Philippine equities ($r = 0.12$), providing variance reduction through cross-border diversification. Meta Platforms (\texttt{META}) and Bitcoin (\texttt{BTC}) receive exact 0.00 percent allocations due to their high standalone annual volatilities (46.80 percent and 68.45 percent, respectively). The GMV portfolio achieves an annualized expected return of 9.42 percent, an annualized volatility of 14.28 percent, and a Sharpe ratio of 0.2917.

  \item \textbf{Maximum Sharpe Ratio (Tangency) Allocation Breakdown}:
    The Tangency portfolio concentrates capital into high-momentum technology engines and power utility stability: \texttt{NVDA} (58.36 percent), \texttt{META} (22.60 percent), and \texttt{MER} (19.04 percent). Domestic telecommunications (\texttt{TEL}) and industrial conglomerates (\texttt{AEV}) are assigned 0.00 percent weights due to lower historical return trajectories. Bitcoin (\texttt{BTC}) receives a 0.00 percent weight because its extreme standalone volatility (68.45 percent) penalizes the portfolio risk denominator relative to \texttt{NVDA}'s superior return-to-volatility ratio. The Maximum Sharpe allocation yields an annualized expected return of 49.82 percent, an annualized volatility of 32.14 percent, and an industry-leading Sharpe ratio of 1.3868.
\end{enumerate}
